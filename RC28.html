Inequality of Opportunity in Brazil: chances of social mobility for highly educated individuals

Research Object and Hypothesis – This article examines inequality of opportunity in Brazil, focusing on the chances of highly educated individuals entering or remaining in the middle class. In the context of the expansion and restructuring of the Brazilian educational system over the last 40 years, there have been significant advances such as the universalization of primary education, the restructuring of secondary and technical education, as well as the expansion and greater fluidity in access to higher education. This expansion and restructuring of the Brazilian education system has also brought advances in the general schooling of the population, with particular emphasis on the significant increase in individuals with completed secondary and higher education. These data are obtained not only in general schooling, but also when we observe the average schooling of each socio-occupational category in Brazil. These related factors raise several questions about the sociological analysis of the relationship between educational credentials and the labor market. That is, whether the educational credentials obtained by individuals guarantee the best chances of upward social mobility for highly educated individuals. The possibilities for conducting such an analysis range from differentiating between income and socio-occupational category, intra- or intergenerational social mobility, as well as choosing a hierarchical or topological analysis. In this sense, this work analyzes the impact of educational credentials on the chances of intergenerational social mobility for the middle class, based on a topological analysis of the occupational categories arranged in the Brazilian social structure. I use the EGP category “Professionals and Administrators” as a parameter for characterizing the middle class. In the Brazilian context, this characterization is established by high schooling, income, and self-identification with this sector of society (Salata, 2015). Thus, the objective is to analyze whether the chances of highly educated individuals entering or remaining in the middle class vary over time. From the analysis of the impact of educational credentials on intergenerational social mobility, I work with two hypotheses: H1) From the perspective of absolute education, educational credentials have lost value over the years; H2) Evaluated from a positional perspective, individuals need new credentials to maintain or enter the middle class (Bills, 2016; Bukodi; Goldthorpe, 2016). These processes are related to previous findings about status barriers (Salata, Sin Yi, 2022; Salata, Sin Yi, 2025) and the credentialist theory of diploma inflation (Collins, 1979), specifically, with the institutional theory of Lester Thurow (1975) and Hirsch (1976). That is, there is a change in the previously observed characteristic of a loss of income enjoyed by holders of higher education degrees in Brazil (Ribeiro, Souza, Carvalhaes, 2010). In fact, a real race is established around more credentials, in order to maintain or enter the best jobs.

Data and Methodology - To achieve these objectives, a quantitative methodology was used, based on the statistical software STATA. This process went through three different stages, which lead to some answers to the main question of this study: a) database construction; b) use of descriptive statistics; c) application of inferential statistics. In the first stage, called database construction, an analysis and aggregation of different databases was carried out, referring to the supplementary part on social mobility of the National Household Sample Survey (PNAD). The application of this survey supplement aims to question respondents about the employment and educational level of their parents. Thus, this study was carried out by aggregating the supplement on social mobility from the surveys of 1982, 1988, 1996 and 2014. The use of this criterion is based on the relationship between the availability of cohort control and the availability of surveys carried out. From the aggregation of these cross-sectional studies into a single database, a reordering of the occupational variables of the respondents and their parents was carried out, based on the EGP occupational typology (Erikson-Goldthorpe-Portocarero, 1979). This typology comprises a series of arrangements for defining the categories used; however, a division into six categories was adopted. For the application of the binomial logit regression statistical technique, a middle-class variable was created, considering the occupational category “Professionals and Administrators”. Despite the numerous discussions about the categorization of the middle class in Brazil (Ribeiro, 2012), based on the use of the EGP typology, I chose to follow the work carried out by (Salata, Scalon, 2012), in a way that encompasses a greater differentiation between individuals in this occupational category regarding income and education obtained. In addition to selecting the occupational variables of the respondents and their parents, based on the EGP typology, we considered the following selection criteria for the data sample for intergenerational comparison purposes: 1) household status as head of household and spouse; 2) age range between 35 and 50 years old (Breen, Muller, 2020). Furthermore, for the construction of educational variables, the CASMIN method of completed years in the education system and the educational transitions/credentials method were used. For the educational transitions/credentials variable, the construction already carried out by Ribeiro (2023) in another study was used. Thus, the variable comprises 4 categories: elementary school I (0 to 4 years), elementary school II (5 to 8 years), high school (9 to 11 years) and higher education (12 years or more). For positional education, criteria were used relating to the position of the most educated to the least educated, which were separated into 4 categories corresponding to the relative frequencies of each year: low, lower-middle, upper-middle, and high education. The total sample consists of 66,254 cases, divided among the four years of the research already mentioned. The second part of the methodology involves the application of descriptive statistics of two orders: 1) cross-tabulation of population study years, either in absolute terms or broken down by occupational type; 2) use of intergenerational mobility tables with analysis of outflows. The aim is to observe in general how data on educational credentials are established over time, as well as to describe the mobility that has occurred in recent decades. Finally, in the third part of the methodology, the inferential technique called Logit Regression (Xie; Powers, 2000) is applied. The use of this technique aims to analyze the chances of upward intergenerational social mobility or the permanence of highly educated individuals in the middle class. Based on this analysis, we related educational credentials – in absolute and positional terms – in order to understand the mechanisms that relate these variables, observing how educational credentials have increased or decreased the chances of social mobility. In addition to the occupational category of Professionals and Administrators, used to observe the chances of remaining in the middle class, we analyzed more closely the chances of the category of Unskilled Manual Workers to ascend to the middle class as ideal types in the formation of the class structure in Brazil. For post-estimation analysis of the data, average marginal effects (AME) were used.

Main Results - The preliminary results corroborate the hypotheses worked on, insofar as they indicate a devaluation of educational credentials and an increased need to obtain more credentials to maintain one’s position in the labor market. This precept relates to research that analyzed the strength of the association between education and social destinies based on the relationship between absolute and relative education (Salata, Sin Yi, 2022). In this study, we do not observe the flow of social mobility itself, but rather the chances of upward mobility through educational credentials in an absolute sense. Despite the increase in the overall schooling of the population, the indices have indicated an increase in inequalities among individuals who achieve the highest levels of education. Observing the category of Unskilled Manual Workers, as an ideal type, we have a 20% drop in the chances of individuals with Higher Education entering the middle class and a 15% drop in the chances of individuals with completed Secondary Education between the years 1982-2014. As for the category of Professionals and Administrators, as another ideal type, we had a drop in the chances of remaining in the middle class of almost 10% for individuals with Higher Education and almost 20% for individuals with Secondary Education between 1982-2014. Considering education as a positional asset, we observe the race of individuals towards a greater number of credentials to maintain or enter the middle class. The results indicate an increase in social closure, especially in 2014, as an even greater number of credentials is needed to overcome the barriers of social origins. With the significant overall increase in schooling, the reduction in the value of a diploma is no longer due to the loss of specific income for individuals who have graduated from higher education, but rather to a mismatch between aspects of educational training and the job market. The late massification of education brings the dilemma surrounding the race for more credentials to enter the best occupations, whether in the alignment between schooling and the job market or in forms of over-education.

Keywords: social mobility; social stratification; educational credential; labor market; positional education.
